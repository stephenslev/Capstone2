Northstar Analytics Employee Experience Policy

Document owner: People Operations
Version: 1.8
Effective date: April 1, 2025
Applies to: All Northstar employees and people managers

Purpose

Northstar aims to create a work environment where employees can perform effectively, develop their skills, raise concerns, and understand how their work contributes to customer outcomes. Employee experience is monitored through surveys, listening sessions, manager practices, workload indicators, and retention patterns.

Listening and surveys

People Operations conducts a quarterly employee experience survey. The standard survey measures satisfaction, manager support, workload sustainability, role clarity, career growth, inclusion, and confidence in leadership. Survey results are reported in aggregate when a group has enough responses to protect confidentiality.

Managers should discuss relevant team themes without attempting to identify individual respondents. Action plans should contain one to three specific actions, owners, and a review date.

Manager expectations

Managers hold regular one-to-one meetings, provide timely feedback, discuss development goals, and address workload concerns. Managers are expected to identify sustained overload, unclear priorities, repeated after-hours work, and team conflicts before they become performance or health issues.

Workload and flexibility

Teams should prioritize work openly and revisit priorities when urgent work is added. Employees may raise a workload concern with their manager, People Operations, or an alternative leader if the direct manager is part of the concern. Flexible work arrangements are supported when they are compatible with customer commitments, security requirements, collaboration needs, and role responsibilities.

Learning and career growth

Employees should have access to role-relevant learning, mentoring, and development conversations. Managers should connect development opportunities to current responsibilities and future capability needs. Career discussions should not depend solely on promotion timing.

Psychological safety and concerns

Employees may raise concerns about conduct, workload, management behavior, process friction, or customer-impacting risks without retaliation. Concerns involving harassment, discrimination, retaliation, safety, or other serious matters should be directed to the appropriate confidential reporting channel.

Policy impact review

People Operations reviews employee experience trends alongside delivery workload, customer escalations, attrition, and business performance. A policy may be adjusted when evidence shows that it creates avoidable friction or prevents employees from serving customers effectively.
